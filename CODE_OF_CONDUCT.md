# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is trying to help. Argue about the code, not
about the person writing it.

## What is expected

- Take the most generous reading of what someone wrote, especially where they
  are working in a second language or a first week.
- Disagree with reasons. "This is wrong because X" is useful; "this is wrong"
  is not, and "you are wrong" is neither.
- Accept a decision once it is made, and say so if you still disagree. Both
  can be true.
- Credit people for their work, including work you are replacing.

## What is not

- Harassment, whether in the open or in someone's inbox. Sexualised language
  or attention. Insults, and the kind that hide behind a joke.
- Attacking somebody for who they are rather than for what they wrote:
  their age, body, disability, ethnicity, gender identity or expression, level
  of experience, nationality, appearance, race, religion, or sexuality.
- Publishing anyone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption — of a thread, a review, or a release.

## Scope

Anywhere this project happens: issues, pull requests, commits, discussions, and
anywhere someone is representing the project in public.

## If something goes wrong

Report it to <oddurs@gmail.com>. Say what happened and where; a link is enough
to start. Reports are read by one person — me — and not shared further than
needed to act on them. You will get a reply.

I will do one of: nothing, with a reason; a private word; a public correction;
or removal from the project. I would rather hear about something small early
than something large late.

Enforcement falls to the maintainer, which is a single person with an ordinary
amount of judgement rather than a committee. If I get it wrong, say so.
